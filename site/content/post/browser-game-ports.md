On October 10, the Instagram account escape reality (@esc8pe.reality) posted a five-slide carousel headlined **"YOU CAN NOW PLAY ALL YOUR FAVE CHILDHOOD GAMES IN YOUR WEB BROWSER."** It picked up 63.9K likes. The second slide is a screenshot of an October 8 tweet from @RadiantOpti that lists browser versions of a dozen well-known games, with links.

This post covers what's on that list, what the source itself admits, and why you shouldn't treat it as a download guide. **We're not reproducing or linking any of the game sites.** Commenters on the original post say several of them redirect to gambling sites or try to install malware.

## The list

Here's everything the tweet names:

1. Call of Duty: Black Ops 1 (Zombies maps)
2. Call of Duty: Black Ops 3 (Zombies maps)
3. Call of Duty: Black Ops 2
4. Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 2
5. Skate 3
6. Halo: Combat Evolved
7. GTA 5
8. GTA: Vice City
9. The Quake series
10. Half-Life
11. Diablo
12. Minecraft
13. An unspecified "and more"

All of these are commercial games, and their copyrights are still held by active publishers. None of the entries is presented as an official release.

## What the source admits

Two caveats come from the post and tweet themselves.

- **"No downloads, no console needed" isn't true across the board.** The tweet says some entries only work if you **already own the game files** and supply them yourself. That's a long way from "just open a tab and play," and the caption skips over it.
- **Publishers are already issuing takedowns.** The caption acknowledges this. These are unauthorized versions, and the people who own the games are treating them that way.

## Treat the links as hostile

This is the part that matters most for anyone tempted to click.

- **Reports of malware and gambling redirects.** Several commenters on the post say the linked sites bounce visitors to gambling pages or try to push installs. A "browser game" that asks you to download something, install an extension, or allow notifications has stopped being a browser game.
- **Mirrors keep changing.** When takedowns hit, links move to new domains, and you have no way of knowing who runs the new copy or what code it serves alongside the game.
- **The "upload your own files" step is a risk of its own.** A site that asks for your game files is asking you to hand local data to an unknown party running unknown code.

If you've already visited one of these sites and installed something, or granted notification or extension permissions, remove it and run a malware scan.

## This isn't preservation

Game preservation is a real and worthwhile cause. It usually means archives, emulation research, or community projects built on legally obtained assets. A viral list of unofficial ports of commercial games, some of them current franchises, with links of unknown origin, isn't that. Don't read "it runs in a browser" as "it's legal" or "it's safe."

## Read the post for what it is

A few details show what the carousel is actually for.

- **Slide 4 says "follow or you'll never see this page again."** That's engagement bait, with no information in it.
- **The account bio pushes a "50 free websites" lead magnet.** This kind of post exists to collect followers and email signups, and it isn't reporting. The game list is the hook.

None of that means every entry is fake. It does mean the post is built for reach, and checking the links wasn't part of the job.

## If you actually want to replay these

Several of the older titles on the list are sold legitimately on mainstream PC storefronts. A few have official re-releases or remasters, and Mojang hosts an official free browser version of classic Minecraft. Some community engine projects also run classic games in a browser, legitimately, using game data you own. Those are worth looking for through the publisher or the project's own official channels, not through a link pulled from an Instagram screenshot.
